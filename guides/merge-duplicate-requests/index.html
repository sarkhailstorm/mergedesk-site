How to merge duplicate requests in Jira Service Management

Jira Service Management can link duplicate requests, but it can't merge them. Here's what that means for your team and your customers, how to merge duplicates by hand, and how to do it properly in one step.

  • Stopping email duplicates
  • Questions

The short answer

  • There's no merge in Jira Service Management. You can link one request to another as a duplicate and close it, but its comments, attachments and people stay where they were.
  • Closing a duplicate usually emails the customer. They get a “resolved” notification, and a satisfaction survey if your project sends one, even though their problem isn't solved yet.
  • Atlassian's suggestion to add merging, JSDCLOUD-4685, has been open since 2017 and has more than 1,200 votes.
  • To really merge, you either copy everything across by hand or use an app such as MergeDesk.

Why duplicates happen

Most duplicate tickets come in by email. Jira Service Management adds an email to an existing request only when it can match it, so small differences create a new request instead:

What the customer didWhat Jira does
Replied from another address, or a colleague repliedCan't match the sender, so it opens a new request.
Typed the ticket key in lower case, like “re: sup-12 still broken”Doesn't spot the key, so it opens a new request.
Said “Thanks!” after the request was resolvedStarts a whole new request for a one-word reply.
Emailed again because nobody answered yetOpens a second request about the same problem.
Had an out-of-office reply switched onTurns the automatic reply into a request nobody needs.

Each duplicate splits the conversation: two agents may answer the same problem, SLAs run twice, and the customer replies in whichever thread they see first.

Merging by hand, without an app

You can get close to a merge with Jira's own tools. Start with the request you want to keep, usually the oldest one or the one with the most detail.

  1. Copy the conversation. Paste the duplicate's description and any useful comments into the request you keep. Add them as an internal note if you don't want to email the customer again.
  2. Move the files. Download the duplicate's attachments and upload them to the request you keep.
  3. Keep everyone informed. Add the duplicate's requester and participants to the Request participants field of the request you keep, so they get its updates.
  4. Link them. Link the duplicate to the request you keep with the duplicates link type.
  5. Tell the customer, then close it. Add a public comment on the duplicate saying where the conversation continues, then resolve it, with the resolution Duplicate if your workflow has one.

Even done carefully, a manual merge has gaps: comments lose their original author and time, the customer usually gets a “resolved” email and survey for the duplicate, and later replies to the old email thread still land on the closed duplicate instead of the request you kept.

Some teams add a Jira Automation rule that closes a request as soon as it's linked as a duplicate. That saves a click, but the comments, files and people still stay split between the two requests.

Stopping email duplicates before they pile up

Merging fixes duplicates after the fact. MergeDesk's duplicate guard also checks each new request against the same customer's recent ones as it arrives. For each rule, an admin chooses whether it merges automatically, suggests a merge to agents, or does nothing.

  • Ticket key in the subject, in any letter case: merged automatically by default.
  • A reply to a request resolved in the last 30 days: merged automatically by default.
  • Same person, same subject, still open: suggested to agents by default.
  • Out-of-office replies and bounces: closed quietly by default.

A matching subject on its own never triggers an automatic merge, and if two emails mention different order or ticket numbers, MergeDesk only suggests. Suggestions appear in the request's Duplicates panel.

Questions

Can you merge tickets in Jira Service Management without an app?

Not really. You can link a duplicate to another request and close it, and copy its comments, files and people across by hand, but Jira Service Management has no merge action of its own.

Does closing a duplicate email the customer?

Usually, yes. Resolving a request normally sends the customer a “resolved” notification, plus a satisfaction survey if your project uses one. MergeDesk closes duplicates without those emails and leaves a note on the portal instead.

Can a merge be undone?

With MergeDesk, yes: for 7 days by default, and your Jira admin can choose up to 30. Undo removes what the merge added, puts back any fields it changed and reopens the duplicates, again without emailing the customer.
